# Page Type Recipes

Fourteen page types. Each: job, query intent, default temperature, section spine (adapt to actual content), proof requirement, riff note, and the common failure. Spines hand off to a layout skill if installed.

---

## 1. Home
- **Job:** carry the brand claim and route every visitor type to their next page.
- **Query intent:** navigational (brand name). **Temp:** T5.
- **Spine:** owned by the layout skill's home recipes. Architecture's only demands: the claim is one sentence, and routing to personas/products is above the second fold.
- **Failure:** trying to close every sale on the home page. Home routes; deeper pages close.

## 2. Product / feature page
- **Job:** prove one capability works, in the buyer's terms.
- **Query intent:** commercial ("<capability> software/tool"). **Temp:** T4.
- **Spine:** capability claim hero, product-in-action (real UI, annotated), 3 to 5 capability sections each with proof, integration band, switch path, CTA.
- **Proof:** real product imagery. A feature page without the product visible is a brochure.
- **Riff:** inherits home's hero shape one notch quieter; may carry the site's signature moment if the product visual earns it.
- **Failure:** feature-name headers that mean nothing to buyers ("Workflow Engine") instead of outcomes.

## 3. Solutions / persona page
- **Job:** make one persona say "this is for people like me".
- **Query intent:** commercial ("<category> for <persona>"). **Temp:** T3-4.
- **Spine:** persona-mirroring hero (their words, their stakes), pain-to-outcome sections in their vocabulary, persona-matched proof (logos and quotes from THEIR world), persona-specific FAQ, CTA.
- **Proof:** at least one customer story from the persona's segment.
- **Failure:** the same page with a different headline per persona. If 80 percent of the content is shared, the personas are not differentiated enough to deserve pages.

## 4. Use-case page
- **Job:** show the product doing one job-to-be-done end to end.
- **Query intent:** commercial ("how to <job>", "<job> tool"). **Temp:** T3.
- **Spine:** job-statement hero, the workflow walked through step by step with product imagery, outcome metrics, related use cases, CTA.
- **Failure:** abstract benefit language where a literal walkthrough should be.

## 5. Pricing
- **Job:** let a serious buyer self-qualify without anxiety.
- **Query intent:** transactional ("<brand> pricing"). **Temp:** T3, clarity outranks flash.
- **Spine:** plans table (highlight one), what-counts explainer (seats, usage), full comparison table, social proof band, objection FAQ (real questions from sales calls), CTA per plan.
- **Failure:** hiding the number. "Contact us" on every tier reads as "expensive and complicated" to most of the market.

## 6. Competitor comparison ("X vs Y")
- **Job:** win an active evaluation honestly.
- **Query intent:** commercial investigation ("<us> vs <them>", "<them> alternative"). **Temp:** T3.
- **Spine:** neutral framing hero (the evaluation is real, respect it), honest feature table (concede where they win), who-should-pick-them section (genuinely), where-we-win section with specifics, switcher testimonials ("I moved from X because..."), migration path, CTA.
- **Proof:** switcher quotes are the highest-value proof on the page.
- **Failure:** trashing the competitor. Readers discount obviously biased comparisons, and honest concessions buy credibility for the claims that matter.

## 7. Alternatives roundup ("best X alternatives")
- **Job:** capture switchers earlier in the search, position us as the obvious first option.
- **Query intent:** commercial investigation. **Temp:** T2-3.
- **Spine:** quick verdict up top (best for whom), our entry first with full case, 4 to 6 honest alternative entries each with best-for framing, comparison table, CTA.
- **Failure:** fake roundups where every alternative is bad. Rank real tools honestly; the page earns trust by being genuinely useful.

## 8. Migration / switch lander
- **Job:** convert an audience actively leaving another tool (end-of-life, price hike, acquisition fallout).
- **Query intent:** transactional ("<tool> migration", "switch from <tool>"). **Temp:** T4, this page closes.
- **Spine:** event-acknowledging hero (name the deadline or change driving the switch), side-by-side mapping (their concepts to ours), import/migration path with effort honesty, switcher proof, urgency from the REAL deadline only, stripped nav variant for paid traffic.
- **Failure:** generic comparison content where switch mechanics should be. The visitor has already decided to leave; show them the door is easy, not why the old tool is bad.

## 9. Integration page
- **Job:** capture "<us> + <tool>" searches and prove the stack fits.
- **Query intent:** commercial, long tail. **Temp:** T2, programmatic.
- **Spine:** pairing hero (both logos, one outcome sentence), what-syncs table, 2 to 3 workflow examples specific to this pairing, setup steps, related integrations.
- **Programmatic rule:** the workflow examples must be unique per integration. If only the logo changes, the page is a doorway.
- **Failure:** building all 200 at once. Ship the top 10 by customer overlap, expand by demand.

## 10. Template / tool / free resource page
- **Job:** capture high-volume informational searches with something genuinely useful, convert a slice.
- **Query intent:** informational-transactional ("<thing> template", "free <tool>"). **Temp:** T2-3.
- **Spine:** the resource itself up top (usable before any gate), what-it-is and how-to-use, the product tie-in (how the real product does this automatically), related resources.
- **Failure:** gating everything. Ungated resources earn links and rankings; the product tie-in converts.

## 11. Case study
- **Job:** let a real customer make the argument.
- **Query intent:** navigational from evaluation journeys. **Temp:** T3 (T4 on studio sites, where work pages ARE the product).
- **Spine:** outcome-first hero (the number, the name, the logo), context (who, stakes), journey (problem, why us, rollout), results with specifics, pull quotes throughout, next-step CTA matched to the reader's segment.
- **Failure:** the press-release case study. "Company X is thrilled to partner" proves nothing; numbers and friction-honest narrative do.

## 12. Pillar / guide page
- **Job:** own the category's biggest informational query and anchor a content cluster.
- **Query intent:** informational. **Temp:** T2.
- **Spine:** definitive-answer opening, navigable table of contents, 6 to 12 chapter sections each linking to a deeper cluster post, diagrams and examples, soft product bridge at natural points.
- **Linking:** the pillar links to every cluster post; every cluster post links back. The pillar earns a nav or hub placement, never buried.
- **Failure:** 8,000 words with no structure. The pillar is a hub to navigate, not a wall to scroll.

## 13. Blog post
- **Job:** answer one question well, feed authority to its cluster.
- **Query intent:** informational. **Temp:** T1-2, typography does the work.
- **Spine:** answer-first opening (respect the searcher), structured body with real examples, one product bridge max, related posts, author with credentials.
- **Failure:** publishing for cadence instead of queries. Ten posts answering real questions beat fifty written for the calendar.

## 14. Company set (about, careers, contact)
- **About:** T3 (T4 for studios and DTC). Founders, why this problem, real faces. Failure: stock-photo culture.
- **Careers:** T2-3. Honest culture content, real roles, what working here is like.
- **Contact / demo:** T2. One clear action, response-time honesty, no 11-field forms. Every field above five costs conversion; ask sales what they actually need.
