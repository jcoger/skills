# Site Map Recipes

Page sets by archetype and stage. Each recipe lists pages with job, temperature, and tranche (1 = launch, 2 = growth, 3 = scale), plus what NOT to build yet. Recipes are starting points: cut pages the team cannot feed with real content, add pages only with a named job and query.

---

## 1. Launch / single product (5 to 7 pages)

For: pre-launch or just-launched products, one offer, small team.

| Page | Job | Temp | Tranche |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home | Carry the claim, convert or capture | T5 | 1 |
| Product (or /how-it-works) | Show the thing actually working | T4 | 1 |
| Pricing (or waitlist/early access) | Remove price anxiety | T3 | 1 |
| About | Who is behind this, why believe them | T3 | 1 |
| Legal set (privacy, terms) | Table stakes | T1 | 1 |
| One launch blog post / manifesto | Linkable story for launch day | T2 | 2 |

Do not build yet: solutions pages, comparison pages, resource centers. A 6-page site that is excellent beats a 20-page site that is thin.

## 2. Seed-stage SaaS (8 to 15 pages)

For: one product, first real customers, starting to compete on search.

| Page | Job | Temp | Tranche |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home | Claim + routing to personas | T5 | 1 |
| Product | Capability proof | T4 | 1 |
| Persona/solutions pages (2 to 4) | "This is for people like me" | T3 | 1 |
| Pricing | Clarity, objection handling | T3 | 1 |
| About | Credibility | T3 | 1 |
| Demo / contact | Low-friction handoff | T2 | 1 |
| Case studies (3+) | Proof that compounds | T3 | 2 |
| Competitor comparisons (1 to 3) | Win the evaluation | T3 | 2 |
| Security / trust | Unblock procurement | T2 | 2 |
| Blog hub + first posts | Feed the persona pages | T1-2 | 2 |
| Integrations hub + top pages | Capture "<us> + <tool>" queries | T2 | 3 |
| Pillar guide | Own the category's big query | T2 | 3 |

## 3. Growth SaaS (30 to 300+ pages)

For: multi-feature platforms, multiple personas, real content capacity. The reference pattern for this recipe is visible on large sales-tech and platform sites (for example apollo.io): platform claim up top, then routing by capability and persona, with a deep programmatic layer underneath.

| Cluster | Pages | Temp | Tranche |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home | Platform claim + routing | T5 | 1 |
| Platform/feature pages | One per capability (4 to 8) | T4 | 1 |
| Solutions | Per persona AND per industry | T3 | 1-2 |
| Use cases | One per job-to-be-done | T3 | 2 |
| Pricing | Plans + comparison + FAQ | T3 | 1 |
| Customers | Hub + case studies by segment | T3 | 2 |
| Competitor set | "vs X" pages + "X alternatives" roundups | T3 | 2 |
| Integrations | Hub + page per integration (programmatic) | T2 | 2-3 |
| Free tools / templates | Programmatic resource pages | T2-3 | 3 |
| Resource center | Blog, pillar guides, academy | T1-2 | 2-3 |
| Trust set | Security, compliance, status | T2 | 2 |
| Company set | About, careers, press, contact | T2-3 | 2 |

Nav rule at this scale: 5 to 7 items with mega-menu panels (Product, Solutions, Pricing, Customers, Resources, Company). The footer is the real sitemap.

## 4. DTC / ecommerce brand

| Page | Job | Temp | Tranche |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home | Brand world + bestsellers | T5 | 1 |
| Collection pages | Shoppable browsing | T3 | 1 |
| Product detail pages | Convert, one per SKU family | T4 | 1 |
| Story / about | The why behind the brand | T4 | 1 |
| Ingredients / materials / process | Substantiate quality claims | T3 | 2 |
| Reviews / results | Aggregated proof | T2 | 2 |
| Journal / editorial | Brand gravity + informational queries | T2 | 3 |
| Support set (shipping, returns, FAQ) | Remove purchase anxiety | T1 | 1 |

Note: DTC inverts SaaS temperature in one place: the story page runs hot (T4). People buy the brand world, not the feature list.

## 5. Studio / agency / portfolio

For: creative studios, agencies, freelancers. The work IS the product, so case studies run hot. Strong public examples of this shape: koto.com and similar brand studios.

| Page | Job | Temp | Tranche |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home | Taste statement + selected work | T5 | 1 |
| Work index | Browsable proof | T4 | 1 |
| Case studies (5 to 12) | The product itself | T4 | 1-2 |
| Services / approach | What engagement looks like | T3 | 1 |
| About / culture | The people and the point of view | T4 | 2 |
| Journal / notes | Thinking in public | T2 | 3 |
| Contact | Start a conversation, fast | T2 | 1 |

Do not build: pricing pages (engagement-priced services sell through conversation), thin service-area SEO pages (they cheapen a taste-led brand).

## 6. Service / local business

| Page | Job | Temp | Tranche |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home | Trust + primary service + service area | T4 | 1 |
| Service pages (one per service) | Own "<service> in <area>" | T3 | 1 |
| Location pages (if multi-location) | Programmatic, unique local data each | T2 | 2 |
| Proof (reviews, before/after, credentials) | Local trust | T3 | 1 |
| About | Faces and history | T3 | 1 |
| Contact / booking | Convert, with real response expectations | T2 | 1 |

Programmatic warning: location pages with swapped city names and identical copy are doorway pages. Each needs unique local proof (jobs done there, local reviews, local team) or it should not exist.

---

## Choosing a recipe

- Match on business model first, stage second.
- Hybrid businesses take the dominant revenue motion's recipe and borrow clusters from the other.
- When auditing a competitor, identify their recipe, then map which clusters they have built out; the clusters they have invested in reveal their acquisition strategy.
