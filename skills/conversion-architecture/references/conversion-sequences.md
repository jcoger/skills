# Conversion Sequences

Argument orders by audience temperature. The sequence is the page's skeleton: each beat answers the visitor's next question. Pick the nearest sequence, then adapt beats to the actual content available.

---

## Audience temperature

Based on the classic five awareness stages (unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, most-aware), collapsed to three working bands:

| Band | Awareness | Typical source | What they need first |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold** | Unaware / problem-aware | Social ads, advertorials, content discovery | To see themselves and their problem before any product appears |
| **Warm** | Solution-aware | Search for the category, comparison queries, referrals | To see why this solution and why you, fast |
| **Hot** | Product-aware / most-aware | Brand search, retargeting, switchers, pricing visits | Mechanics, price, reassurance, and a clean path to act |

The cardinal sin is sequence-temperature mismatch: educating hot traffic about a problem they have already paid to solve, or closing cold traffic that does not yet trust you. Both read instantly as "this page is not for me".

## The master sequence

The full argument, in the visitor's natural question order. Every other sequence is this with beats cut or compressed:

1. **Identity match.** "This is for people like me." The visitor's situation in their words.
2. **Trust signal.** Early, fast: who else trusts this (logo band, scale number).
3. **Product reality.** The thing itself, shown working. Not metaphors.
4. **Proof.** Outcomes with names and numbers.
5. **Mechanics.** How it actually works; effort honesty.
6. **Offer.** What you get, what it costs, framed concretely.
7. **Objections.** The top hesitations, answered directly.
8. **Remaining questions.** FAQ for the tail.
9. **Close.** The primary CTA with risk reversal restated.

Cold traffic gets all nine. Hot traffic might get five. The beats keep their relative order; temperature decides which ones exist and how long each runs.

## Sequences by temperature

### Cold (advertorial / discovery traffic)
Identity match (long: the problem narrated in their words) -> problem agitation (what it costs them today) -> the better-way turn (category-level, why old solutions fail) -> product introduction (late! the product appears after the visitor is nodding) -> proof -> mechanics -> offer -> objections -> close.
Notes: the product appearing too early is the most common cold-traffic failure. Cold pages run long; length is not the enemy, boredom is.

### Warm (category search / comparison shoppers)
Claim hero (the sharpest version of why you) -> immediate trust band -> product reality (show it working in the first two viewports) -> differentiated proof (why you over the obvious alternatives, with numbers) -> mechanics compressed -> offer -> top 3 objections -> close.
Notes: warm visitors are comparing tabs. Speed to "what makes this different" decides whether your tab survives.

### Hot (brand search / switchers / retargeting)
Acknowledge their state (returning visitor, switcher event, or brand promise restated) -> mechanics and price up front (they came for specifics) -> reassurance proof (switcher quotes, security, support reality) -> objections specific to acting now -> close with real next-step transparency.
Notes: hot pages fail by withholding. Hiding price or burying mechanics from someone ready to act sends them back to search.

### The direct-response LP pattern (warm-hot paid traffic)
A proven public pattern visible on large SaaS direct-response landers (for example Intercom's DRLPs): claim hero with one CTA pair -> mass trust line ("trusted by 25,000+ brands") -> benefit triad split by audience role -> capability sections per role, each with the same CTA pair -> named testimonials with specific outcome numbers -> closing CTA band.
Why it works: one repeated CTA pair (trial + demo, primary + lower-commitment), proof with names and percentages, audience self-selection without separate pages. What it typically omits (and where you can outconvert it): objection handling and price transparency.

### Pricing page sequence
Plans table (one highlighted) -> what-counts explainer (the unit of pricing in plain language) -> proof band (social proof adjacent to the money ask) -> objection FAQ from real sales calls -> per-plan CTA.
Notes: clarity outranks persuasion here; a pricing page that needs heavy selling means earlier pages failed.

## Lander taxonomy (paid and campaign traffic)

Named direct-response page styles and when each wins. Useful as a shared vocabulary when briefing:

| Style | Temperature | When it wins |
|---|---|---|
| Advertorial | Cold | Story-led discovery traffic; reads as editorial before it sells |
| Better Way | Cold-warm | Category has a hated incumbent habit to push against |
| Explainer | Warm | Product is genuinely novel; understanding IS the conversion barrier |
| Reasons Why | Warm | Listicle-framed argument ("7 reasons teams switch"); skimmable persuasion |
| Us-vs-Them | Warm-hot | Active comparison shoppers; pairs with honest comparison rules |
| Influencer / UGC | Cold-warm | Trust borrowed from a known voice; proof-led from the first beat |
| New Product | Warm-hot | Launch moments; novelty itself is the hook |
| Bundle / Offer | Hot | Deal-led closing for an audience that already wants the thing |
| Quiz | Cold-warm | Personalization earns the pitch; the result page is the lander |
| Seasonal | Mixed | Real calendar relevance (honest urgency built in) |

Match ad intent to lander style: competitor-keyword traffic to Us-vs-Them or migration pages, problem-keyword traffic to Explainer or Better Way, brand traffic to the shortest hot sequence. Sending paid intent to the homepage wastes the targeting.

## Sequence rules

- Every beat earns the next scroll; if a beat does not advance the argument, cut it.
- The first viewport always contains: identity or claim, one trust signal, the primary CTA.
- Proof never sits more than one viewport from the claim it supports.
- The close restates the offer and risk reversal; never just a button on a colored band.
