# Objection Mapping

Why visitors hesitate, how to find out, and where on the page each hesitation gets answered. The page that answers the visitor's actual objections converts; the page that answers imagined ones decorates.

---

## Harvesting objections (in order of signal quality)

1. **Sales calls and demo recordings.** The questions prospects actually ask, in their words. The single best source; the pricing FAQ should be a transcript of these.
2. **Lost-deal reasons.** Why people said no; the objections that actually kill.
3. **Support and onboarding tickets.** The friction that exists after purchase predicts the fear before it.
4. **Review mining, yours and competitors'.** Competitor 1-star and 3-star reviews are a map of category-wide anxieties; your own reviews show what surprised people.
5. **Chat logs and search queries on your site.** What visitors ask when nobody is watching.
6. **Team brainstorm.** Last resort, clearly labeled as hypothesis until validated.

## Ranking

Score each objection frequency x severity (how often it arises x how often it kills the deal when it does). The top 5 get sequence positions on the page; the rest go to the FAQ tail or deeper pages.

## The six objection families

| Family | Sounds like | Strongest answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Price / value** | "Too expensive", "cheaper option exists" | Reframe against cost of the problem or the incumbent; what-counts pricing clarity; ROI numbers from proof |
| **Trust / risk** | "Will this actually work for us?", "is this company real?" | Segment-matched proof, guarantees, security answers, real company signals |
| **Effort / migration** | "Switching is painful", "no time to learn a new tool" | Effort honesty with specifics ("most teams migrate in one afternoon"), import paths, white-glove offers |
| **Timing** | "Not now", "maybe next quarter" | Real cost of waiting (honest only); low-commitment alternate CTA to keep the thread |
| **Fit** | "We're too small / too big / different industry" | Persona-matched proof and explicit fit statements ("built for teams of 5 to 50") |
| **Authority** | "I need to convince my boss / team" | Shareable one-pagers, ROI calculators, security docs; sell the champion the tools to sell internally |

## Placement: answer where it arises

The map assigns each top objection a position just before it would naturally surface:

- Fit objections arise at the identity beat: answer them in the hero and persona framing.
- Trust objections arise at the first claim: answer with the early proof band.
- Effort objections arise at the mechanics beat: answer inside it, never after.
- Price objections arise at the offer: answer beside the price, not in a separate FAQ.
- Timing and authority objections arise at the close: answer at or just before the final CTA.

Inline answers beat FAQ answers for the top 5: a visitor who carries an objection through three more beats is half-lost already. The FAQ is for the tail (questions 6 through 15), and FAQ content should be real questions verbatim from sales calls, not marketing questions the company wishes people asked.

## Worked map (condensed)

Mid-priced team SaaS, warm traffic, demo conversion:

~~~
1. "Will my team actually use it?" (fit+effort, kills most deals) -> mechanics beat: 2-minute setup walkthrough + adoption stat
2. "How is this different from <incumbent>?" (trust) -> differentiated proof beat: side-by-side with switcher quote
3. "What does it really cost at our size?" (price) -> offer beat: what-counts explainer + calculator
4. "Is our data safe?" (trust/risk) -> badge row + security link adjacent to the form
5. "Can I convince finance?" (authority) -> close: ROI one-pager download as alternate CTA
Tail -> FAQ: contract terms, support hours, integrations list
~~~

## Rules

- Never invent an objection the audience does not have; answering it plants it.
- Never answer an objection with an adjective; every answer needs a mechanism or a proof.
- Revisit the map quarterly; objections shift as the market and product change.
